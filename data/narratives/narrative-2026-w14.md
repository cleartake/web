Seven consecutive negative periods. Then the streak broke.

The aggregate index rose 0.85% this week — modest in isolation, but meaningful after seven straight weeks of declines that dragged the index to its all-time low. The prior week had been the worst since the February selloff, with the aggregate falling 3% and Platform dropping over 4%. That kind of follow-through after the SMCI shock suggested the market might be entering a deeper correction. Instead, it bounced.

All three layers finished positive, with Application leading at roughly 1%, followed by Compute and Platform just below. March 31 was the standout day — 46 of 49 companies finished green in a broad relief rally, led by Marvell (+13%), ON Semiconductor (+11%), and Super Micro's own bounce (+8%). For the first time in eight periods, the concentration pattern also flipped: both the top 5 and the remaining 44 companies contributed gains. The narrow-leadership dynamic that had defined the market for nearly two months finally broke, if only for a week.

The more telling development is what happened to bubble readings. For the first time this year, Platform overtook Application as the most elevated layer. Platform's bubble index climbed to 0.45, driven by a sharp jump in valuation acceleration to 0.65 — up from 0.46 just two weeks ago. Application's reading actually fell from 0.49 to 0.41 as its prolonged selloff finally started pulling expectations back toward fundamentals.

This is worth pausing on. For months, Application carried the highest speculative readings while Compute was the cleanest. Now the picture is shifting: Compute's bubble index has risen to 0.42 (from 0.35 three weeks ago), and Platform has the most elevated reading of any layer. The market's pain trade may be rotating.

Year-to-date, Compute has given back most of its outperformance. It's up just 1.4% — down from nearly 10% at its peak in mid-February. Platform and Application have converged at roughly -15% each. The 20-point spread between Compute and the downstream layers that defined February has narrowed to about 16.

One week doesn't make a bottom. But after seven weeks of uninterrupted selling and the index's worst level on record, the bounce — broad-based, with concentration breaking and bubble readings starting to normalize in Application — carries more signal than a typical dead-cat rally. The question is whether the macro backdrop allows it to continue. Tariff uncertainty, export control scrutiny, and a hawkish Fed remain overhead. The floor is visible. Whether it holds is another matter.
