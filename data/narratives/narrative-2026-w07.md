The rally kept going. The aggregate index climbed another 3% this week, extending the rebound from January's selloff. All three layers posted gains — the third consecutive all-positive period this year, after none in January.

But the more interesting story is who led. For the first time since late December, it wasn't Compute. Platform and Application both outpaced infrastructure this week, with Platform gaining about 3.6% and Application 3.1%, compared to Compute's 2.4%. After six weeks of Compute dominance, the recovery is starting to rotate downstream.

Earnings set the tone again. Datadog surged nearly 14% on Tuesday after crushing estimates, reinforcing that cloud infrastructure spending is flowing through to Platform companies — not just hardware makers. Unity told the opposite story, plunging 26% on Wednesday despite beating on revenue. Weak forward guidance and fears around Google's Project Genie spooked investors. Unity continued falling through Friday, a reminder that Application remains the layer where disappointment gets punished hardest.

Year-to-date, Compute still leads comfortably at around 10%. Platform has climbed back into positive territory. Application remains the only layer still underwater for the year, though the gap is narrowing. The aggregate is now up about 3% — a sharp reversal from its negative reading just two weeks ago.
Bubble readings barely moved. Compute ticked up slightly but remains in normal territory. Application eased a touch, though its valuation acceleration is still the highest of any layer — something worth watching if the price recovery stalls.

Two weeks ago, the question was whether January's selloff was the start of something deeper. The answer, for now, is no. The index has recaptured its losses and the recovery is broadening beyond Compute. The next question is whether Platform and Application can sustain this momentum on their own fundamentals, or if they're still riding infrastructure's wake.
