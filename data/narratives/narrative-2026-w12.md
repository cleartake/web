The stall broke — and it broke in the one place the market didn't expect.

The aggregate index fell 2.7%, ending the three-week drift and pushing the index to its lowest level since inception. But the real story is which layer led the decline. For the first time this year, Compute was the worst performer — down over 4%, its steepest weekly drop since December. Platform fell 2.6%. Application, the perennial laggard, actually held up best, losing just 1.3%.

The catalyst was company-specific but the implications are structural. Super Micro Computer plunged 33% on Friday after the DOJ charged its co-founder with smuggling $2.5 billion worth of NVIDIA AI servers to China in violation of export controls. The arrest — part of a scheme that used shell companies in Southeast Asia to disguise shipments — dragged the broader Compute layer with it. NVIDIA fell 4% for the week, Intel dropped 5%, and Micron shed nearly 5%. Only 7 of 49 companies finished Friday positive.

This matters beyond the headlines because SMCI accounts for roughly 9% of NVIDIA's revenue. The smuggling charges raise questions about how much of the compute "demand" that has supported the infrastructure thesis was legitimate domestic deployment versus export-controlled shipments finding unauthorized routes to China. It doesn't undermine the broader constraint story — hyperscaler capex commitments are real and enormous — but it introduces a new category of risk to the layer that had been considered the safest.

The concentration pattern continued its streak. For the sixth consecutive period, the top 5 contributors posted gains while the remaining 45 (now 44, with one company dropping out of Platform) lost ground. Narrow leadership has now been the default state for nearly two months.

Year-to-date, Compute's lead has shrunk dramatically. It's still positive at roughly +3%, but that's down from nearly +10% just three weeks ago. Platform sits at -12%. Application at -14%. The Compute-Application spread — which hit 20 points at its widest — has narrowed to 17 as infrastructure gave back ground.

Bubble readings held mostly steady. Application remains the most elevated at 0.49 with valuation acceleration still at 0.80. The aggregate ticked up to 0.41 — the highest reading this year — not because expectations are rising, but because prices are falling faster than speculative indicators can adjust.

For eleven weeks, Compute's resilience was the defining pattern of 2026. Infrastructure held up in every selloff, every rotation, every panic. This week tested that assumption. Whether the SMCI scandal is an isolated governance failure or the leading edge of a broader export-control reckoning will determine whether Compute's outperformance resumes — or whether the one layer the market trusted is starting to show cracks of its own.\
