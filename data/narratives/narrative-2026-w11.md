The volatility is gone. The headlines aren't. The aggregate index slipped just 0.3% this week — essentially flat — after similarly small declines the prior two weeks. Since the double shock in Week 08, the market has posted four consecutive negative periods, but the magnitude has collapsed from nearly -5% to virtually nothing.
This isn't recovery. It's exhaustion. The index has settled at its lowest level since October 2025, and nothing in the past three weeks has been strong enough to reverse or extend the selloff.

Even NVIDIA's blowout earnings couldn't change the direction. The company reported $68 billion in revenue — beating estimates comfortably — and guided higher than expected. The stock popped after hours, then gave it all back over the following two sessions. In a healthier market, those numbers would have been a catalyst. In this one, they were absorbed without a ripple. Compute lost roughly 1% over the three weeks since the selloff, far less than the other layers, but even infrastructure couldn't pull the broader index higher.

The most striking feature of this period is what's happening underneath the headline number. The top 5 contributors have been positive in every period since the selloff began — four consecutive periods of narrow leadership carrying the index while the remaining 45 companies lose ground. This is the same concentration pattern we saw in January, but the context has shifted. In January, the top 5 were driving gains. Now, they're preventing deeper losses.
Adobe's earnings tell the Application layer's story in miniature. The company beat on revenue and earnings, but fell 8% after its long-tenured CEO announced he would step down — and investors continued punishing the stock over AI disruption fears. Oracle jumped 9% on strong cloud results the same week. Within the same layer, the market is aggressively sorting winners from the disrupted.

Bubble readings are diverging in a way that deserves attention. Application's bubble index climbed to 0.49 — its highest reading this year — with valuation acceleration at 0.80, far above any other component in the index. This means that despite falling prices, Application valuations are still running ahead of fundamentals. Platform's readings, by contrast, have fallen to 0.35 as its selloff has brought expectations more in line.
Year-to-date, the divergence keeps widening. Compute leads at roughly +7.5%. Platform is down nearly 10%. Application is down over 13%. That's a 20-point spread between the top and bottom layers — the widest of the year.

Three weeks of near-zero aggregate movement can feel like nothing is happening. But the calm masks a deeply split index: infrastructure holding firm, software under sustained pressure, and a concentration pattern that's keeping the headline number from reflecting how much stress sits underneath.
